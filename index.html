This is not legal advice

Ambit is an educational and record keeping tool. It is not legal advice, it is not a substitute for advice, and your use of it creates no lawyer client relationship. Nothing here is an advertisement for, or a solicitation of, professional work.

The tool records the answers you give and sets the relevant provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, the DPDP Rules 2025 and the GDPR beside them. It does not apply law to your facts and reaches no conclusion about your compliance. Every judgment in the finished assessment remains yours.

Two limits are worth stating plainly. Significant Data Fiduciary status cannot be self assessed. It arises only on notification by the Central Government under s 10(1), and nothing here confers it. And most of the Indian framework is not yet in force: sections 3 to 17 of the Act, and rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 of the Rules, commence on 13 May 2027.

The law changes. Verify every provision against the gazetted text current at the date you rely on it, and take qualified professional advice before acting on anything recorded here.

Where the two regimes part company

Three points of departure, each one changing what a controller or fiduciary must actually do.

Identical processing, different obligations.
QuestionDPDP Act 2023GDPR
What sets the duty off DPDPThe duty reaches a Significant Data Fiduciary alone, and that status is conferred by notification of the Central Government under s 10(1). Identical processing carries no assessment duty until then. GDPRArticle 35(1) self executes. Where processing is likely to result in a high risk, the controller must assess it before processing begins. No one has to designate anything first.
Whether sensitivity has its own regime DPDPThere is no special category regime at all. Sensitivity enters only through the s 10(1) factors the Government weighs, and through what counts as reasonable under the rule 6 safeguards. GDPRArticle 9 prohibits processing special categories absent a listed condition, and Article 10 governs offence data. Large scale processing of either is a named Article 35(3)(b) trigger.
What happens at unmitigated high risk DPDPNothing ex ante. The Board has no clearance function and no provision requires a fiduciary to approach it first. Rule 13(2) instead reports significant observations after the fact. GDPRArticle 36(1) requires the controller to consult the supervisory authority before processing, where the assessment shows a high risk that mitigation has not brought down.

A fourth runs underneath all of them. Chapter V prohibits transfers by default and permits them by mechanism, while s 16(1) permits them by default and lets the Government restrict them by notification.

How the scoring works

Most impact assessments reduce a risk score by however many controls happen to be ticked. Ambit does two things differently.

Controls reduce likelihood, never severity

Severity passes through untouched

Encrypting a store does not make the harm to a principal smaller if it spills. It makes the spill less likely. So a control moves a risk left across the matrix and never downward, and the residual position you see is the honest one.

Coverage is counted per risk

Only controls mapped to a risk reduce it

Every risk carries a theme and every control is mapped to the themes it addresses. A risk that nothing in scope addresses is reported as unmitigated, rather than quietly scoring as covered because unrelated boxes were ticked elsewhere.

Commencement

Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 of the DPDP Rules 2025 came into force on publication of G.S.R. 846(E) on 13 November 2025. Rule 4 follows one year after publication, and rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 eighteen months after it, on 13 May 2027 (r 1(2) to (4)). Sections 3 to 17 of the Act are enforced from that same date by a separate notification, G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025. The assessment and audit duty in r 13(1) therefore bites from May 2027, running once in every period of twelve months from the date on which a fiduciary is notified as significant.
